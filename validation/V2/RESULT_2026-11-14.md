## What failed, and the likely cause of each

All causes below are read from the V2 outputs; none is tested yet.

### Mark 1: 9 missed loci, all in *Citrobacter*

rsmA (x4), PmrF (x2), mdtB (x2), msbA (x1). The reads for these genes are present and the
pipeline sees them, but the genes are rejected for identity. Examples from the 8x wastewater
mixture (gene identity to the CARD protein): mdtB 0.8995, PmrF 0.8959, rsmA 0.8879. The cut is
0.90. In the pure genome the same genes sit at 0.92 (mdtB), 0.906 (PmrF) and 0.897 (rsmA). The
*Citrobacter* copies are about 89-92% identical to the nearest CARD protein, which is right
on the line. Random sampling of reads moves a gene across it: the same gene passes in one sample and fails in
the next. msbA was rescued by the "or" rule (high_identity) in one sample and missed in others.

Likely cause: the 0.90 gene-identity cut falls in the middle of where real orthologs from an
under-represented genus lie. This is a rule-threshold problem, not a pipeline fault.

### Mark 2: 14 unexplained detections

13 are in *Citrobacter* (pure genome: AcrF 152 reads, mdtH 77, mdtA 58, KpnG 36; mixtures: MdtK,
mdtA, ramA, AcrE, acrA with 2-13 reads), plus IreK in *Enterococcus* (3 reads, 9% covered).
These are real *Citrobacter* efflux-pump genes with no RGI Perfect/Strict hit, so the test
calls them unexplained. AcrF in the pure genome has 0.858 read identity but passes through the
averaged gene identity (0.902). The same genes fail in other samples (AcrF at 0.884 in the 8x
mixture). The ones that detect are those whose reads happened to average above 0.90.

Likely cause: the same boundary as mark 1, seen from the other side. At the same threshold,
distant orthologs that are not RGI Strict get called in some samples. Note the 3-read IreK call
is a different, small case: three reads covering 9% of the gene pass the count and breadth-ratio
tests by chance.

Honest reading: marks 1 and 2 cannot both be met by moving this one threshold. Lowering it
rescues the 9 misses and adds more unexplained calls; raising it removes the unexplained
calls and adds more misses. The "or" rule helped on rounds 3 and 4 because those genomes had
orthologs further from the threshold.

### Mark 3: 8 outliers

- AAC(6')-Ie-APH(2'')-Ia in *Enterococcus*: observed is 5.6-5.9x the truth in the pure genome and both 8x
  mixtures. The truth locus lies on contig NZ_JCAWFM010000002.1 at a simulated depth of 2.9,
  while the chromosome is about 11.6 in the same sample. Likely cause: the truth takes the
  depth of one locus, and reads arrive from a second, unlisted copy of the gene or from a high-copy
  element. Same pattern as AAC(6')-Ib10 in round 4. Needs checking against the genome (a BLAST of
  the gene against all seven contigs) before it is called a truth error.
- rsmA (x4 samples), PmrF and mdtB (8x wastewater): observed 0, because the gene was not
  detected (mark 1's misses). These are the same nine events, counted again.

So mark 3 has one separate problem (the AAC(6')-Ie copy count) and the rest is mark 1 again.

## Decisions for the user

Each option ends with one recommendation. None has been started. A rule change makes V2's
frozen rule history, so any change means a new design and a V3 with a fresh genome draw.

### D32. Do not change the rule for the three V2 fails, or change it?

- A. Keep rule "or" as is, report V2 as a failed validation, and publish nothing. The rule
  works on rounds 3 and 4 and fails on a genus with divergent orthologs.
- B. Change the rule's identity handling (see D33) and run V3 on a fresh draw.
- C. Stop trying to reach "0 misses, 0 unexplained" in protein mode and redefine the claim
  (see D34): protein mode reports "present, with an identity band", not "detected yes/no".

Recommended: C, with B kept as the later fallback. The threshold cannot satisfy marks 1 and
2 on genomes whose genes sit at 89-92% identity, so the rule is not the problem; the pass mark is.

### D33. If the rule is changed, what changes?

- A. Lower the gene identity cut from 0.90 to 0.85. Rescues the 9 misses, but adds many more
  unexplained calls (every distant ortholog).
- B. Replace the yes/no cut with a band: report genes at 0.85-0.90 as "probable homolog",
  at 0.90 or above as "detected". Marks then score only the "detected" class.
- C. Leave the rule alone.

Recommended: B. It keeps the existing detected set (so rounds 3 and 4 results stay valid) and
stops a coin flip at the boundary deciding yes or no.

### D34. Should marks 1 and 2 be re-defined for protein mode?

- A. Keep them as written (100% found, 0 unexplained).
- B. Score the identity band: mark 1 only on loci at 0.92 identity or above to CARD; mark 2
  only on genes above 0.90 in the "detected" class. Genes in the 0.85-0.92 gap are reported, not marked.
- C. Drop marks 1 and 2 for protein mode and report the numbers only.

Recommended: B, and only if the user agrees it is a pre-registered change made before V3, not
after V2's result is hidden. Dropping a mark that failed (C) would be the wrong direction.

### D35. The AAC(6')-Ie-APH(2'')-Ia overcount (mark 3)

- A. Check the genome first (BLAST all seven contigs); if there is a second copy, fix the truth.
- B. Leave it as a known limitation.

Recommended: A. It is cheap, needs no Docker and decides whether this is a truth error.
